# Techserva: idea audit

No flattery. This is what is weak about the idea and what the MVP does about it.

## The biggest problems

1. **No proof.** The site lists 500 services and has zero clients, zero case studies and zero reviews. A price list does not create trust. Fix: sell the small, checkable items first (roasts, audits, one-page sites), then publish real results with permission. The site invents nothing in the meantime.
2. **500 services is a claim that can backfire.** A serious buyer reads "pen tests and Unity games and pitch decks" as a shop that does nothing well. Fix: keep the catalog as search and SEO surface, and lead with a few categories in outreach.
3. **Capacity.** One operator with an AI agent cannot run 50,000 clients at once. The queue can hold 50,000 records; ChatGPT rate limits and human review set the real limit, likely tens of active jobs. Security, legal-adjacent and app store work need a qualified person to sign off.
4. **"Paid in full before delivery" scares new buyers.** With no track record, buyers carry all the risk. The rule protects the provider, and it will cost sales. Fix to test later: a smaller paid stage 1 that buyers can walk away from.
5. **USDT and USDC only.** This suits crypto-native and cross-border buyers. Most Indonesian small businesses expect bank transfer or QRIS. Expect low conversion there until local rails exist.
6. **Machine translation in 226 languages.** Some of it is wrong. For example, the Arabic for "Technical SEO setup" reads as if it were about chief executives. Only the English text binds, but a bad translation still costs trust in that language. Fix: human review for the top 10 markets first.
7. **Prices are unproven.** Entry prices come from the owner's market table. International prices are a formula, not market research per country.
8. **Marketplaces already do this.** Fiverr, Upwork, Sribu and Projects.co.id have reviews, escrow and traffic. The wedge here is narrow: fixed scope and written terms on one page, without a bidding process.

## Business model canvas

| Block | Current answer | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | Small firms and founders who want a fixed price without a sales call | Too wide; pick two segments per market |
| Value | Public price, written scope and terms before any conversation | Copyable; no moat yet |
| Channels | SEO from 500 service pages in 226 languages, known contacts, marketplaces | SEO is slow; machine-translated pages may rank poorly |
| Relationship | Self-serve page and assistant, then one person | Handoff depends on one operator |
| Revenue | Fixed-price orders, hosting and maintenance renewals | Renewals are the only recurring part |
| Key resources | Catalog, 50,000 prompt templates, terms library | Templates are not experience |
| Key activities | Delivery, review, support | Review is the bottleneck, not generation |
| Partners | Subcontractors for security, mobile, games | None signed |
| Costs | AI usage, subcontractors, domain | Low now; subcontractors squeeze margin at entry prices |

## SWOT

- **Strengths:** low cost to run, public pricing, terms ready for each service, any language can read the catalog.
- **Weaknesses:** no clients, no team, no legal entity on the terms, one operator.
- **Opportunities:** long-tail search for specific small jobs, cross-border buyers who already hold stablecoins, repeat hosting and care plans.
- **Threats:** marketplaces, cheap AI builders, refund disputes without escrow, liability from security work.

## What would change the verdict

Ten paid orders in one category with published results would prove more than any further work on this site. Until then, treat this as a tested storefront and not as a proven business.

## Rejected from the brief, with reasons

- **Web3 and Solana integration:** the brief marks that block as an example and it does not fit a services shop. USDT and USDC payment is kept.
- **A 99.5/100 or 95/100 score:** no scorer exists, so none is claimed.
- **A Turnitin figure under 5%:** cannot be promised.
